The owner supplies the artwork for the first three Sless shirt designs; Printify prints and ships them. The owner asked the agent to select the additional 50 merchandise items from researched best-selling and trending Printify products.

**Why:** The owner explicitly chose “I’ll make the artwork; Printify prints and ships them” and repeated “You looked up the best sellers and trendy products. Do those.”

**How to apply:** Do not ask the owner to supply a 50-product list again. Select from the actual catalog and current evidence, without claiming precise sales rankings that the sources do not provide. Do not create duplicate listings within a shop or replace the owner's original three designs. A controlled copy into the connected shop is allowed while preserving the originals. Do not treat product mockup photos as print-ready artwork.

Include Halloween, Thanksgiving and Christmas themed merchandise for the upcoming seasons.

**Why:** The owner explicitly asked to add those holiday themes if they were not already in the assortment.

**How to apply:** Include all three themes in the merchandise assortment alongside year-round products. Keep the owner's original three shirt designs unchanged. Do not promise holiday arrival dates without current production and shipping verification.

asked for designs that are “more modern, fun and something people want to buy.”

**Why:** The owner directly corrected the creative direction after seeing the
live assortment.

**How to apply:** Prioritize distinctive illustrations, playful concepts, and
strong product-specific compositions instead of repeatedly applying the same
stacked slogan and small icon. Preserve the owner's original three artworks.
Do not claim a design is a proven bestseller merely because it looks appealing.

**Why:** The owner said “I like the ring side riot” and “do more of the person

Keep the approved Sless Taylor mascot and add a separate full-beard variant;
do not replace the approved character with the bearded version.

**Why:** The owner said “the mascot is good” and “Also add a version of this beard”

Treat beard and outfit treatments as alternate versions. The owner subsequently
requested “A little less facal hair,” but rejected the light-stubble treatment as
“not enough hair.” Aim between the original full beard and stubble: a defined,
close-trimmed beard with connected moustache and clear jaw/chin coverage, without
the original bulk. The merchandise concepts remain subject to the owner's
requested tweaks before live replacement.

For Halloween, both boot soles must rest ON TOP of the top turnbuckle pad in a
low ledge-like crouch, not dangle beside the post. His free hand rests in his lap,
his expression is serious or scary, ropes are orange, and he wears white/black/

**Why:** The owner explicitly corrected the rejected pose: “have his feet on top

white/black/silver gear photo. Earlier generic crouches did not meet the request.
The owner accepted the feet-on-top direction as “better,” then explicitly asked
for a smaller, more realistic turnbuckle and closer feet and knees.

**How to apply:** Show actual sole-to-top-pad contact and the ring post below his
feet. Preserve the approved likeness and keep the pumpkin lantern in the other
hand. Do not treat sitting beside a pad as the requested perch.

Keep separate Halloween apparel and canvas wall-art versions. The shirt artwork
must have a shaped transparent perimeter rather than a square printed panel.
When listed, the shirt must offer purple, orange, and black options. Canvas
wall art must use the exact approved full-square haunted-scene design, NOT the
shirt-adapted artwork.

**Why:** The owner explicitly requested an easier-to-print shirt background and

**How to apply:** Preserve the approved square composition unchanged as the
canvas master; make apparel adjustments only on a separate copy. Do not silently

requirements and available variants before making the products purchasable.

For front-printed shirts, prioritize a clearly printed front view of every color
instead of mixing unlabeled blank backs into the color gallery.

**Why:** The owner interpreted blank back mockups as black and orange shirts
missing their artwork. Changing a featured color can also leave another color's
front absent from the gallery even though its variation image remains correct.

**How to apply:** After changing featured images or republishing supplier mockups,
check that every offered color has a printed front in the gallery and the correct
variation image. Omit blank backs or label them unmistakably as unprinted backs.

Size raster-based merchandise by the original source's effective print density,
not just the uploaded template's pixel count or DPI metadata.

**Why:** Resampling the approved concept to supplier template dimensions does not
create original detail. Larger product options can make an unchanged design
unsuitably soft even when the uploaded file looks large enough.

**How to apply:** Keep source resolution and physical artwork dimensions explicit
when adding sizes; obtain a higher-detail approved master before offering formats
that require it. Do not silently enable larger variants or regenerate the canvas
composition to satisfy a pixel-count check.

The owner approved the sharpened upscale of the existing Halloween shirt art
at approximately 10.5 inches wide on purple, black and orange.

**Why:** After reviewing supplier mockups against the smaller print and a
close-up sharpness comparison, the owner chose “Approve and update all three colors.”

**How to apply:** Treat this larger apparel treatment as approved; preserve the
existing character, composition and shaped transparent perimeter rather than
redrawing them. Do not describe the enhanced upscale as native high-resolution
source detail or as physical print-quality verification. The canvas remains the
separate exact-original design.

For precise AI pose corrections, a rejected full mockup can anchor the generator
to the same wrong anatomy and ring geometry even with detailed negative prompts.

**Why:** Repeated edits preserved the rejected floating/straddling pose and old
cape color despite explicit corrections.

**How to apply:** Use a fresh visual pose guide plus isolated approved-face and
gear references when geometry remains wrong, then inspect contact points before
presenting. For localized corrections, overpaint the rejected body geometry with
the intended stance before asking for a finished rendering. Do not claim a pose
requirement is satisfied based on the prompt alone.

The three original shirts must be US-only. The additional 50 products should be available wherever Printify supports shipping for each product.

**Why:** The owner said “Wherever printly ships to. The 3 shirts that I make and manage will only be usa” and approved a WooCommerce checkout rule to enforce the limit.

**How to apply:** Do not assume Printify's broader shipping profiles enforce the owner's restriction. Block non-US checkout for core shirts, including mixed carts, while retaining product-specific Printify availability for the other merchandise.

The owner requested adding the media service to the website, making it live, and finishing the merchandise. This authorizes work toward launch; it does not resolve expired giveaway dates, inconsistent bundle savings, missing print files, pricing, or an unverified checkout/fulfillment path.

**Why:** The owner explicitly said “add this to my website, make it live and finish the other merchandise” and later “Let's get the rest of the merch up so i can start selling.”

**How to apply:** Do not keep safe launch work draft-only merely because the earlier redesign was draft-only. Preserve unrelated WordPress content and footage, and withhold unsupported offers and unverified sales flows. Replit publication still requires the owner's Publish action.

A Printify product’s `visible` flag is not proof that it is published on the website or able to fulfill orders.

**Why:** Visible products can remain in a shop whose sales channel is disconnected.

**How to apply:** Verify the intended sales-channel connection, public storefront, checkout and order synchronization before claiming merchandise is live.

Preserve the legacy static Checkout page when introducing native WooCommerce checkout. Use the native checkout's configured URL, not a guessed `/checkout/` route.

**Why:** An existing page can reserve the desired slug, causing WooCommerce to create a different checkout permalink.

**How to apply:** Read the configured WooCommerce page and its actual public link before wiring payment, cart, or thank-you navigation. Do not overwrite an unrelated existing page to resolve the collision.

A blank Printify return popup does not establish that native store connection failed, and connecting a new channel does not automatically migrate the old shop's catalog.

**Why:** A return URL indicated success while its popup was blank; the account API confirmed a new WooCommerce shop, with the original shirts still in the old disconnected shop.

**How to apply:** Verify the account's shops and each shop's products before recommending another connection attempt or deletion. Preserve original products and plan a controlled transfer to the connected shop.
